Aggregation of Marginal Progress (AMP)

Small improvements. Big results.

AMP

Aggregation of Marginal Progress (AMP) is the idea that becoming significantly better at anything doesn't require a dramatic breakthrough in any single area. Instead, it's the result of making small, consistent improvements across many areas of performance. In the pool that includes technique, strength, power, conditioning, efficiency, mindset, and consistency.

Each improvement may seem insignificant on its own, but together they reinforce one another and produce meaningful gains. The goal isn't to become great at one thing, but to become a little better at everything.

As a team, we aspire to the AMP philosophy. Small improvements. Big results.

An Example

Consider a swimmer who currently swims the 100 freestyle in 1:10.00.

Over the course of the season, she makes modest improvements across a number of areas (shown below). She now has the potential to swim a 1:06.25 without having made an extraordinary breakthrough in any single area.

Area Race Improvement Cumulative Race Improvement
Start 0.15 sec 0.15 sec
Breakout 0.10 sec 0.25 sec
Turns 0.50 sec 0.75 sec
Finish 0.20 sec 0.95 sec
Stroke Efficiency 0.80 sec 1.75 sec
Body Position 0.70 sec 2.45 sec
Technique Under Fatigue 0.80 sec 3.25 sec
Pain Tolerance 0.50 sec 3.75 sec

It's not all physical

It's important to recognize that not all the aforementioned improvements are physical. We consider them to be from the following five categories.

  • Technical Improvements: Better streamlines, turns, breakouts, body position, catch, and finishes.
  • Physical Improvements: Greater strength, power, mobility, coordination, and general athleticism.
  • Physiological Improvements: Better aerobic capacity, speed, muscular endurance, and recovery.
  • Mental Improvements: Greater confidence, willingness to exert maximum effort, concentration, and race composure.
  • Behavioral Improvements: Better attendance, consistency, sleep, preparation, and commitment to practice.

The Multiplication Effect

Swimming involves many interconnected abilities. Improving one area can make improvements in another area even more valuable. The benefits aren't always independent; they reinforce and amplify one another.

  • Better strength allows a swimmer to generate more force.
  • Better technique allows her to apply that force more effectively.
  • Better conditioning allows her to sustain that force longer.
  • Greater confidence allows her to access more of her physical capabilities.
  • Better turns allow her to maintain momentum and minimize lost time.

You don't need one big breakthrough. You need lots of little improvements.

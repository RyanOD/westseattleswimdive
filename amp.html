Accumulation of Marginal Progress (AMP)

Small improvements. Big results.

AMP

Accumulation of Marginal Progress (AMP) is the idea that becoming significantly better at anything doesn't require a dramatic breakthrough in any single thing. Instead, it's the result of making small, consistent improvements across many areas of performance.

Each improvement may seem insignificant on its own, but together they reinforce one another and produce meaningful gains. The goal isn't to become great at one thing, but to become a little better at many things.

We are guided by the AMP philosophy in and out of the pool. Small improvements. Big results.

The AMP Swimmer

Consider a swimmer who currently swims the 100 freestyle in 1:10.00.

Over the course of the season, she makes modest improvements across a number of areas (shown below). She now has the potential to swim a 1:05.45 without an extraordinary breakthrough in any single area.

Area Improvement Cumulative Improvement
Start 0.15 sec 0.15 sec
Breakout 0.50 sec 0.65 sec
Turns 0.50 sec 1.15 sec
Finish 0.20 sec 1.35 sec
Stroke Efficiency 0.80 sec 2.15 sec
Body Position 0.70 sec 2.85 sec
Improved Race Execution 0.50 sec 3.25 sec
Technique Under Fatigue 0.80 sec 4.05 sec
Pain Tolerance 0.50 sec 4.55 sec

Progress Comes in Many Forms

Notice how not all her improvements happened in the pool or the weight room. Becoming a better swimmer requires growth across five areas, each contributing to overall performance. AMP encourages athletes to pursue progress in all five.

  • Technical Improvements: Better streamlines, turns, breakouts, body position, catch, and finishes.
  • Physical Improvements: Greater strength, power, mobility, coordination, and general athleticism.
  • Physiological Improvements: Better aerobic capacity, speed, muscular endurance, and recovery.
  • Mental Improvements: Greater confidence, willingness to race, concentration, and race composure.
  • Behavioral Improvements: Better attendance, consistency, sleep, preparation, and commitment to practice.

The Multiplication Effect

The five areas of improvement are interconnected. Progress in one can enhance progress in another: strength makes technique more effective, conditioning helps sustain performance, and confidence allows athletes to better use their physical abilities. AMP gains reinforce one another, multiplying their combined impact.

AMP - Small improvements. Big results.
